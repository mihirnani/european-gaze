Old Persia and Old India

Sanson, Nicolas, 1600-1667; Sanson, Guillaume (1633-1703) · 1702

A paired sheet of ancient Persia and India Vetus from the Sanson family’s atlas of ancient and modern geography. India is reconstructed through classical regions, peoples and rivers and placed beside Persia as an object of learned comparison.

Ancient and modern geography

The Sanson atlas deliberately placed classical geography beside contemporary maps. This sheet contains two ancient worlds on one page: the Persian and Parthian empires at left and ‘Old India’ at right. It belongs to a publication whose title page is dated 1697 but whose individual maps extend into the following decade.

Reconstructing India from texts

India Vetus uses the names and divisions of Greek and Roman geography rather than those of the early eighteenth century. Rivers, regions and peoples are arranged as scholarly evidence. The map is consequently historical even though it is drawn with the conventions of contemporary French cartography.

Comparison as method

The same publishing house could map India twice: as the Mughal polity of the present and as the India of classical authors. Knowledge was understood diachronically. To know a country meant to compare what ancient texts said with what modern geographers believed.

The gaze

India is approached from the library. The map treats the subcontinent as an object of European erudition, recoverable through textual criticism and capable of being placed within a universal history of empires.
