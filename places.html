Places

The Deccan’s cities, forts, rivers and coasts, with their older spellings, and the sheets in the collection on which each is found.

Each entry gives the name as the collection uses it, the modern name, the spellings met on European sheets, the entries in the Deccan collection that belong to the place, and the maps, by year and room. The map list is drawn from four sources, and each link says which. Linked from the Deccan collection: the Deccan entry for that place names the sheet among its related maps. Named in the notes: the sheet’s own page here – its title, brief, commentary or caption – names the place, in one of the spellings listed. Within the sheet’s region: only the sheet’s region tag (Bombay, Malabar, the Deccan) puts the place inside its frame. Seen on the sheet: the place was found on the plate itself, named in one of the spellings listed, or drawn and labelled, or – for a region – the subject of the sheet.

The index began as a record of what the notes say, not what the plates show; in August 2026 the links that rested on the notes alone were checked against the plates, and the labels above record the result. Corrections are welcome: a name seen on a plate, a spelling not listed, a sheet that belongs under a place – to mihir [at] naniwadekar [dot] in. For the spellings and how to read them, see How to read one of these.

Notes on method

On 26 August 2026 every link that rested on the notes or the region tag alone was checked against its plate: those that held were kept and now also say seen on the sheet; ten, where the commentary had mentioned a place in passing, were removed. On the same twenty-nine sheets the places plainly legible at the plate’s own size were added under that label. What remains unchecked is the links taken from the Deccan collection’s own curation, which were not read against the plates, and the thirty-nine sheets no notes-derived link led to. A place with no maps under it is a place neither the notes nor that pass has reached.
